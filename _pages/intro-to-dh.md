**ENG 237-W: "Introduction to Digital Humanities"**

As a field that straddles computation and humanistic thinking, 
Digital Humanities (or DH) has always been in flux as new technologies enter 
the DH toolbox and raise new questions. The field is now undergoing another shift as LLMs (Large Language Models) 
make existing computational tools both more accessible and more opaque and introduce new ways of engaging with digital text. 
This course will offer an overview of where the field has been and where it is going, exploring questions like:
--“What constitutes humanistic data?” 
--“What are the limits and opportunities of computational modeling of texts for literary and cultural analysis?” 
--“What are ethical and environmental concerns associated with DH?” 
--“How does DH understand and interrogate generative tools like LLMs?” 
While no prior coding or digital humanities experience is required, students will practice coding through guided online workbooks. 
They will use this practice to develop and carry out a semester-long research project using a digital tool and accessible online archives. 
Two in-class essays, a revised final essay, and article presentations will help students connect hands-on work with the course’s theoretical debates.
